Most local businesses keep fixed hours. Customers don't. They remember they need a dentist while brushing their teeth at night. They notice the boiler making a noise on Sunday afternoon. They plan Saturday's haircut on Thursday evening, when the kids are finally in bed.

That means a real share of the people who want to buy from you try to get in touch when nobody is there to answer.

## What usually happens

After hours, a call goes to voicemail or rings out. A message on Instagram or WhatsApp sits unread until the morning. An email waits in an inbox with forty others.

From the customer's side, this looks like silence. Some will try again tomorrow. Many won't. They'll search again, find a business that replies, and book there. Nobody tells you this happened, so it never feels like a loss.

## What customers actually want at 9pm

They rarely want a long conversation. Usually it's one of three things:

- **An answer.** "Are you open Saturday?" "How much is a filling?" "Do you do gluten-free?"
- **A time.** "Can I come in tomorrow morning?"
- **Reassurance.** "Someone will get back to me about this."

If all three can be handled at 9pm, the customer goes to bed with a booking, and you start the next day with it already in your calendar.

## What a good after-hours answer looks like

A good after-hours response is honest about what it is and useful straight away:

1. It answers from your real information: your services, prices and opening hours. If the answer isn't in your information, it says so instead of guessing.
2. It offers times that are actually free in your calendar, and only says "booked" once the booking exists.
3. For anything it can't handle, such as an emergency, a complaint or a special request, it tells the customer what happens next and passes it to you with the details.
