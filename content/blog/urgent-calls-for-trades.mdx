If you run a trade, your phone rings most when you can least answer it: under a sink, on a roof, halfway through a fuse board. And the calls that come in then are often the most valuable ones. Someone has water coming through the ceiling. Someone is locked out with a child inside. They aren't going to wait for a callback.

At the same time, plenty of calls aren't urgent at all: a quote for next month, a question about a boiler service, "do you cover my area?"

The problem is telling them apart without stopping work every time the phone goes.

## Two kinds of call, two kinds of answer

**Urgent calls** need to reach you, now, with the details: what's wrong, where, how to call back. The customer needs to hear that someone will get back to them quickly, and when.

**Routine calls** need an answer and a booking: your area, your call-out charge, your next free slot. These don't need you at all, as long as whoever answers knows your business.

## What to set up

1. **Decide what counts as urgent.** Water leaks, no heating in winter, a lockout, a burning smell. Write it down.
2. **Decide how you want to hear about it.** A text or WhatsApp to your own phone, with a summary, is usually best on a job.
3. **Write down the routine answers.** Areas you cover, call-out fees, how quotes work, typical lead times.
4. **Be honest about timing.** "We'll call you back within the hour" is better than a promise you can't keep.
