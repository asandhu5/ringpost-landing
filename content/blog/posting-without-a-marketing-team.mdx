Ask any local business owner about social media and you'll usually hear the same thing: "I know I should post more." Customers check your Instagram or Facebook before they book. A page that hasn't been updated since spring makes people wonder if you're still open.

The problem isn't knowing what to post. It's finding the time, every week, on top of running the business.

## What actually works for local businesses

You don't need viral videos. For most local businesses, what works is simple and regular:

- **Your services, shown clearly.** What you do, what it looks like, what it costs to find out more.
- **Your place and your people.** The shop, the van, the team. Customers like to know who they'll meet.
- **Timely posts.** A weekend slot that's free, a seasonal service, holiday hours.
- **Proof.** Before and after, finished jobs, happy customers (with their permission).

## Consistency beats perfection

Three decent posts a week, every week, do more than one beautiful post a month. The goal is to stay visible and look active, so that when someone thinks of what you do, they think of you.

## Keep it looking like you

Generic stock images don't help. Posts should look like your business: your colours, your logo, your real services. Your own photos are best of all, even quick ones from your phone.

## A simple weekly rhythm

If you're starting from nothing, try this for a month:

- **Monday:** a service you offer, with what it includes.
- **Wednesday:** your people or your place, a real photo if you can.
- **Friday:** something timely, such as a free slot, a seasonal job or weekend hours.

Three posts a week is enough to look active. Once the habit is there, add more of whatever gets the most comments and messages.

## Captions that do some work

A caption doesn't need to be clever. It should say what the post is about, who it's for, and what to do next: "Book by message", "Reply for a quote", "Call us today". Every post is a small invitation, so make it easy to accept.
