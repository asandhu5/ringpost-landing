Customers reach local businesses in more ways than ever: a phone call, a text, a WhatsApp, an Instagram DM, a Facebook message, an email. Ask most owners which of these brings in the most work and you'll get a guess.

Guesses are fine until you have to decide where to spend time and money. Should you post more on Instagram? Is it worth promoting your WhatsApp number? Is anyone actually calling?

## Count people, not just messages

One customer might call on Monday, then WhatsApp on Tuesday to confirm. That's one person, not two. If you count every conversation as a new customer, every number looks bigger than it is.

## Follow the conversation to the booking

The useful question isn't "how many messages did we get?" It's "how many of the people who contacted us booked?" That means linking each booking to the conversation that made it, and seeing where people stopped: they asked a price and never replied, they were offered times and didn't pick one, or they booked.

## Be careful with "this post brought customers"

It's tempting to say a post worked because messages arrived the same day. That's a coincidence, not evidence. Real evidence looks like this:

- The customer messaged by tapping a link or button on the post itself.
- The same account commented on the post and then sent a message.

Anything else should be counted as "not known", not credited to whichever post went out last.

## What to do with it

Once you can see which channels bring bookings and where people drop off, the next steps are usually obvious: answer faster on the channel that converts best, follow up the people who asked a question and never booked, and stop spending time on what doesn't bring anyone in.
