Every restaurant knows the moment. The dining room is full, the kitchen is busy, and the phone starts ringing. Someone has to leave a table to answer it, and half the time it's a question about opening hours or whether there's a vegan option.

Meanwhile, more guests try to book by message: Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook. Those wait until someone has a minute, which on a busy night is never.

## What callers usually want

- **A table.** For how many, what time, which night.
- **Information.** Hours, the menu, dietary options, parking, whether you take walk-ins.
- **Changes.** Moving or cancelling a booking, adding a guest.
- **Something special.** A birthday, a large group, a private room.

Most of these have the same answers every time. Only the last one usually needs a manager.

## Answering without leaving the floor

The aim is simple: every guest gets an answer, and no one on the floor has to stop to give it.

1. **Put your real information in one place.** Hours, menu highlights, dietary options, group policies.
2. **Take bookings against real availability.** No double-booking the window table.
3. **Send the special requests to a person.** With the details, so they can reply when there's a moment.

## Write down the questions you get every night

Spend one evening noting every call and message that isn't a booking. Most restaurants find the same ten questions again and again: do you have a high chair, can you do nut-free, is there a corkage fee, how late is the kitchen open, can we bring a cake. Once those answers are written down, nobody needs to stop and give them in person.

## Decide what a "special request" is

Make a short list of what should always go to a manager: groups above a certain size, private hire, allergies you can't guarantee, complaints. Everything else can be answered and booked straight away. A clear line keeps the floor staff out of the phone queue, and makes sure the requests that matter still get a human answer.
