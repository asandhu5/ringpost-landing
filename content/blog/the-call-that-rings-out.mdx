Every local business has a version of the same afternoon. You're with a customer, or halfway up a ladder, or elbow-deep in a colour. The phone rings. You can't get to it. By the time you look, there's a missed call from a number you don't know, and no voicemail.

That caller was probably a new customer. And there's a good chance they've already called someone else.

## Why it doesn't feel expensive

A missed call doesn't show up anywhere. There's no line on a report for "work that went to someone else". Nothing breaks. The day carries on. That's exactly what makes it the most expensive thing that happens to a business that lives on its phone: it's invisible.

The people who ring a local business are usually ready to act. They need a cut on Saturday, a leak looked at today, a table for tonight. They're not browsing. If nobody answers, the next result on the list is one tap away, and most people won't wait for a callback they're not sure is coming.

## Voicemail isn't the answer

Voicemail made sense when people expected to leave a message and wait. Today most people would rather not leave one, especially for a business they've never used. Even when they do, you listen to it hours later, call back, and reach their voicemail. The conversation that would have taken two minutes turns into a day of phone tag.

## What actually works

The fix isn't a louder ringtone. It's making sure every call gets a response, right away, whatever you're doing. That can mean three things, and a good front desk does all of them:

1. **Answer the call.** If a person can't, a voice that knows your services, prices and hours can, and can book the appointment there and then.
2. **Follow up a missed call in seconds.** A text from your business number ("Sorry we missed you, can we help?") turns a ring-out into a conversation the caller can carry on while they're still thinking about it.
3. **Hand over what needs you.** Some calls need a person: an emergency, a complaint, a question nobody has written an answer to. Those should reach you immediately, with the details, not sit in a queue.
